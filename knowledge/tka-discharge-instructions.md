# Total Knee Replacement — Surgeon Discharge Instructions

SYNTHETIC SAMPLE CONTENT FOR A PROOF OF CONCEPT. NOT FOR CLINICAL USE.

## Driving
Do not drive for at least 4 weeks after surgery. Never drive while you are taking opioid pain medicine such as oxycodone. Your surgeon will confirm at your 2-week follow-up visit when it is safe for you to start driving again.

## Showering and bathing
You may shower 48 hours after surgery as long as your waterproof dressing is in place. Pat the dressing dry after showering. Do not soak your knee in a bath, hot tub or swimming pool until your incision is fully healed and your surgeon says it is okay, usually at least 4 weeks.

## Wound care
Keep your waterproof dressing on until your 2-week visit unless it is leaking or peeling off. Do not put creams, lotions or powders on the incision. Some bruising and mild warmth around the knee is expected. Check your incision every day for increasing redness, drainage, or edges opening, and report these in your daily check-in.

## Swelling and ice
Swelling in the knee and lower leg is common for several weeks. To help with swelling, elevate your leg with your ankle above the level of your heart for 20 minutes several times a day. Apply an ice pack wrapped in a towel for 20 minutes at a time, 3 to 4 times a day. Do not place a pillow directly under your knee; support your heel and calf instead. Report swelling that is getting worse in your check-in.

## Walking and activity
Walk with your walker or crutches as your physical therapist showed you. In the first week, take short walks of 5 to 10 minutes, 4 to 6 times a day. Increase your walking a little each day as you feel able. Avoid sitting for longer than 1 hour at a time. You may put as much weight on your leg as is comfortable unless your surgeon told you otherwise.

## Home exercises
Do your home exercises 2 to 3 times a day: ankle pumps, quadriceps sets, heel slides, and straight-leg raises. Your physical therapist will add exercises as you progress. Some discomfort during exercises is normal, but stop and rest if pain becomes sharp.

## Physical therapy (PT)
Outpatient physical therapy begins within the first week after you go home, usually 2 to 3 sessions each week for 6 to 8 weeks. Bring your walker and wear loose shorts to your PT sessions. Your physical therapist will measure how far you can bend and straighten your knee.

## Sleeping position
Sleep on your back with your leg straight for the first 2 weeks. You may place a pillow under your calf and heel, but not under your knee. Sleeping on your non-operated side with a pillow between your knees is okay if it is comfortable.

## Stairs
Go up the stairs leading with your non-operated leg, and down the stairs leading with your operated leg. Always use a handrail.

## Blood clot prevention
You will take a blood thinner after surgery to help prevent blood clots. Do ankle pumps every hour while you are awake and keep walking regularly. Wear your compression stockings during the day for the first 2 weeks.

## Return to work
Most people with desk jobs return to work 4 to 6 weeks after knee replacement. Jobs that involve standing, lifting or physical labor may need 3 months. Discuss your return-to-work date with your surgeon.

## When to contact your care team
Contact your care team the same day if you have a temperature of 101°F or higher, increasing redness or warmth around the incision, drainage from the incision, pain that keeps getting worse despite your medicine, or new swelling in your calf.

## Call 911
Call 911 for chest pain, trouble breathing, coughing up blood, fainting, or sudden confusion.
