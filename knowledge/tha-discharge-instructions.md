# Total Hip Replacement — Surgeon Discharge Instructions

SYNTHETIC SAMPLE CONTENT FOR A PROOF OF CONCEPT. NOT FOR CLINICAL USE.

## Hip precautions
For the first 6 weeks, follow your hip precautions: do not bend your hip past 90 degrees, do not cross your legs, and do not turn your operated leg inward. Use your reacher and sock aid instead of bending down.

## Driving
Do not drive for at least 4 to 6 weeks after hip replacement, and never while taking opioid pain medicine. Your surgeon will tell you at your follow-up visit when you may drive.

## Showering and bathing
You may shower 48 hours after surgery with your waterproof dressing in place. Use a shower chair and a long-handled sponge so you do not bend past 90 degrees. Do not soak in a bath, hot tub or pool until your surgeon approves.

## Wound care
Leave the waterproof dressing in place until your follow-up visit unless it is leaking. Check the incision daily for increasing redness, drainage, or opening, and report changes in your daily check-in.

## Swelling and ice
Swelling of the thigh and lower leg is common. Lie down and elevate your legs for 20 minutes several times a day. You may use an ice pack wrapped in a towel on the hip for 20 minutes at a time.

## Walking and activity
Walk with your walker 4 to 6 times a day, starting with 5 to 10 minutes and building up gradually. Avoid low chairs and soft couches. Sit in a firm chair with arms.

## Home exercises
Do ankle pumps, gluteal squeezes, and heel slides 2 to 3 times a day, staying within your hip precautions.

## Physical therapy (PT)
A home or outpatient physical therapist will start working with you in the first week after discharge, usually 2 to 3 sessions each week.

## Sleeping position
Sleep on your back with a pillow between your legs for the first 6 weeks. If you sleep on your side, lie on your non-operated side with a firm pillow between your knees.

## Blood clot prevention
Take your blood thinner as prescribed, do ankle pumps every hour while awake, and wear compression stockings during the day.

## When to contact your care team
Contact your care team the same day for a temperature of 101°F or higher, increasing redness or drainage at the incision, a sudden change in the length or position of your leg, or pain that keeps getting worse.

## Call 911
Call 911 for chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, or sudden confusion.
